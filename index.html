        Method transfer · equivalence · TOST

        Is the new lab the same as the old one?

        An ordinary t-test cannot answer that: failing to find a difference is not proof of sameness, it is often just too little data. TOST asks the right question — is the difference small enough to ignore? — and gives a yes, a no, or an honest "not yet".

        Variance components · one-way ANOVA · Gage R&R

        Is that spread the process, the supplier, or the assay measuring it?

        A result moves and everyone has a theory. This splits the total spread into named sources and says what share each one owns — so you know whether to go after the vendor, the method, or nothing at all. The same arithmetic, pointed at your own instrument, tells you whether the assay can even see the differences you are chasing.

        What the data should look like

        With both spec limits given, the verdict is judged against the tolerance — the question a QC lab actually has. Without them it is judged against the spread of the parts in this study, which flatters a study made of very different parts and punishes one made of similar ones.

        Advanced settings

        Tukey's method holds that confidence across every pair at once. Running separate t-tests on ten pairs gives you roughly a one-in-two chance of a false alarm somewhere, which is how arguments about a blameless lot start.

        Tolerance intervals · power · sample size

        What limits can this data actually defend — and how much data would it take?

        The mean plus three standard deviations is not a specification: on ten batches it covers far less than people think, because the standard deviation is itself a guess. A tolerance interval does the honest version, and the same arithmetic run backwards tells you how many batches or results a study needs before it is worth running.

        Sequence optimizer

        Codon optimization by genetic algorithm. Paste a coding DNA sequence and it evolves synonymous codons — same protein, different spelling — to raise the Codon Adaptation Index for your host, hold GC content inside its window, keep the 5' end unfolded, and remove restriction sites. It runs in a background thread, so the page stays responsive. Learn how this works ↗

        Keep the start unstructured

        A hairpin is a short run of bases pairing with a run just downstream, closing over a loop. Across the first few codons these hold up ribosome loading, so one there can cost more expression than a poor codon choice further in. Ticking this makes it a hard requirement rather than something traded off: a sequence that still has one never wins, whatever its CAI. Stems are counted in Watson–Crick pairs over a loop of 3 to 8 bases — a short run pairing across a 30-base gap is not a hairpin. There is little room to work with here (the start codon is fixed and only a few codons follow), so if it cannot be cleared the report says so rather than quietly dropping the requirement.

        How your data stays private

        • Nothing is uploaded. Your file is read by your own browser. All math (models, DOE, charts) runs in JavaScript on your device.
        • The browser is told to block every outgoing connection. This page carries a security policy with connect-src 'none', so even a bug or a bad add-on in this code could not send your data anywhere.
        • Nothing is stored, and you can delete on demand. No cookies, no local storage, no database. Press Finish and delete my data when you are done. Data is also wiped automatically after 30 idle minutes, when you leave the page, or when you close or refresh the tab.
        • No AI service sees it. The agent is built from classic statistics and machine learning plus written rules. No LLM, no API key, no prompts sent anywhere.
        • No trackers. No analytics, ads, web fonts or third-party scripts.
        • Works offline. Once the page has loaded you can switch off Wi-Fi and it still works.

        Fine print: like any website, the host (GitHub Pages) sees that the page was opened. It never sees your data. Browser extensions you install can read any page, so use a clean browser profile for sensitive data.
